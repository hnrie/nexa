NEXA - PRIVACY POLICY
Site: chat.evilion.xyz
Run by: Nora (@nora in the app)
Contact: evilionx3@proton.me
Effective: 2026-09-24

This policy lists exactly what Nexa holds. It is written against the
actual database, so if something is not listed here, there is no place
for it and nothing to hand over.

1. THE SHORT VERSION

- Your messages, files, and calls are encrypted end to end. Nobody who
  runs Nexa can read them, and nobody monitors what you do in a chat.
- What the owner and staff can see is your public profile, public
  images, the delivery details the server needs (who sent what,
  where, and when), and moderation records: the actions taken on an
  account, and the case threads with Nexa that go with them.
- No email, no phone number, no real name, no payment details, no
  location, no contacts, no analytics, and no push tokens are ever
  collected.

2. WHAT IS ENCRYPTED (NOBODY WHO RUNS NEXA CAN READ IT)

- Message text and edits. Stored only as ciphertext.
- File contents, file names, file types, dimensions, and per file
  keys (the key travels inside the encrypted message).
- Call audio and video. Calls are not recorded and are never written
  to disk.
- Your settings (themes, notification choices, mutes). Only the size,
  the version number, and the last written time can be seen.
- Your password and recovery key. Your password never leaves your
  device; your browser only sends a derived check value. Your recovery
  key is shown once in your browser and never stored anywhere.
- Your private identity key and your conversation keys. Your private
  key sits on the server only wrapped by your password or recovery
  key. Conversation keys sit on the server only sealed separately to
  each member. Nobody who runs Nexa holds a usable key of any kind.
  There is no spare key, no master key, and no escrow.

There is no setting, admin action, or software change that would
decrypt content already sent, and no way to deliver future content in
readable form.

3. WHAT THE OWNER AND STAFF CAN SEE

a) Profile information (public by design, typed by you): username,
   display name, profile picture and banner, about me, pronouns,
   accent colors, custom status and expiry, presence preference, DM
   privacy setting, per server nicknames and pictures, account ID,
   creation and last change times, and admin and disabled flags. None
   of this is identity checked.

b) Public images (stored as uploaded, can be handed over if ordered):
   profile pictures, banners, server icons, custom emoji, stickers.

c) Delivery details (needed to send and notify, not content): message
   ID, channel, author, type, timestamps, edit time, reply target,
   mentions (user, role, and everyone flags are kept outside the
   ciphertext so the server knows who to notify), file presence and
   sizes, reaction values and counts, pins, read positions, and poll
   vote positions (not the question or option text, which is
   encrypted).

d) Membership and structure (typed by users, stored as typed):
   friends, requests, and blocks, server memberships and roles, DM and
   group participants, channel names, topics, and structure, invite
   codes you made (uses and expiry, but not who used them), bans, and
   per community audit log entries.

e) Sessions: one entry per signed in device. That is the session ID,
   the IP address noted once at sign in (never updated), the browser
   or app description, and the sign in and last active times. Staff
   see when an account was last active in the moderation panel.
   Entries are deleted on sign out or expiry. This is the only place
   an IP address is written. A locked subchat also notes which
   sessions typed its password (the password itself is not kept; it
   only unlocks the screen, and messages stay encrypted under the
   conversation key). Guest call links belong to the link, not to
   your account.

f) Login checks (values not handed over, listed to close the point):
   the password check value, the password stretching salt and
   settings, your public key, your wrapped private key (cannot be
   opened without your password), and the wrapped recovery copy with
   its check value. None of these is a password, and none can be
   turned into one.

g) Moderation records (not end-to-end encrypted, kept as the record
   of how Nexa is moderated):
   - Moderation cases: which action was taken on an account, the
     reason, which member of staff took it, when, how long it lasts,
     and the values a reset replaced (for example the old username).
   - Case threads: the messages between a member and staff in a
     moderation case, stored in readable form and readable by every
     member of staff.
   - The moderation log: an append-only record of every staff action
     (who, to whom, what, why, and when), with the names as they were
     at the time. Nobody can edit or delete it.
   - Held usernames: a username reset by staff stays attached to its
     account, so it still signs that account in, and nobody else can
     register it.
   - Announcements from Nexa, with who wrote them.
   - Whether you have seen each notice, and how far you have read
     your messages from Nexa.

h) Day to day running: unfinished upload progress (expected and
   received sizes), crash reports (error kind, code location, app
   version, and message, never any chat or file content), one site
   wide monthly data total for calls (not per account), and server
   request logs (page, result, size, and duration, with no IP
   address recorded there).

4. WHAT IS NEVER COLLECTED

No email address. No phone number. No legal name, birth date, age, or
ID documents. No payment details. No registration IP or last known IP
field beyond the per session sign in IP above. No IP or access
history. No online time history. No call records (who called whom,
for how long). No device or advertising IDs and no push tokens. No
location. No address book imports. No search words, link clicks,
analytics, or tracking of any kind. No drafts, backups, or unsent
copies. Search runs on your device, using messages it already
decrypted.

5. WHO SEES WHAT

- Other people see your public profile, your messages once their app
  decrypts them, and the communities and channels you share with
  them.
- The owner and the staff she appoints see sections 3a to 3h. Staff
  use a moderation panel that shows public profiles, when accounts
  were last active, moderation cases and their threads, the log, and
  announcements. It shows nothing that is encrypted, because nothing
  here can decrypt it.
- No third parties, no advertisers, and no analytics companies.
  Nothing is sold or shared, except as forced by a valid legal order
  (see Terms section 5), and even then only what exists above.
  Encrypted content still cannot be handed over.

6. HOW LONG THINGS ARE KEPT

- Sessions: until you sign out, or after 90 days without use.
- Messages and files: until they are deleted, by their author, by a
  conversation or server owner, or with the author's account when
  staff delete it.
- Unfinished uploads, and files nothing points at any more: removed
  after a day.
- Crash reports: only the newest 500 are kept.
- Moderation cases, case threads, held usernames, and the moderation
  log: kept as the moderation record, including after an account is
  deleted.
- Backups, if kept for disaster recovery, hold the same encrypted
  content under the same limits.

7. DELETION

- Deleted messages, files, and accounts are removed from the
  database. No trash bin or archive is kept.
- Deleting your own account overwrites its profile, and deletes its
  sessions, relationships, settings, key grants, and second factor
  rows. Your past messages stay behind, still encrypted, marked as
  written by a deleted user.
- When staff delete an account, everything it posted and uploaded is
  removed with it, everywhere, and servers and group chats it owns
  are deleted for everyone in them. The moderation case, its thread,
  and the log entry stay.
